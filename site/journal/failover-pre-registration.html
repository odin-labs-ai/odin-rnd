EXP 021: Kill the database mid-session, does anyone notice?

EXPERIMENT NOTE / 010 · EXP 021 · LAB 03 (FAILOVER) · PRE-REGISTERED 25 OCT 2026 · AMENDED 25 OCT 2026 · NOT YET RUN

EXP 020 left one question for later: what happens when the database's main server dies? This is that study, Lab 03 (failover). While the agent space writes its proof records and a feed turns them into marks on the lens, the primary fails. We ask which database keeps every write it confirmed and the feed intact, with the shortest write outage the client can see and the fewest kinds of moving parts, when each gets its best realistic high-availability design.

There is no overall winner. Each fault gets its own verdict, and the moving parts get theirs. The two contenders are a MongoDB 8.0 replica set of three data members, and PostgreSQL 16 with Patroni 4.x and etcd 3.5.x on three nodes. The plan went through two rounds of review, each with one reviewer arguing for MongoDB's best design and one for PostgreSQL's. All 22 of their vetoes were accepted. All four reviews are published below.

Status: pre-registered, not yet run. This plan was written, hashed and merged to main before any counted run. Counted runs start only after the commit that publishes it. Nothing in it may change afterwards, except by a dated amendment that is published first.

The five faults

Each fault strikes at a seeded instant, and each waits for full recovery before the next.

F1b is a labelled line: it is published and never decides a verdict.
FaultWhat happens
F1, node deathdocker kill -s KILL of the primary's node container
F1b, process death (labelled)Only mongod / the postmaster is killed
F2a, full isolationThe primary is disconnected from both networks, app (the client) and ha (peers and replication)
F2b, peer-only partitionThe primary is disconnected from ha only: the client still reaches it

What is measured

  • The gate: 0 acknowledged writes lost (every acknowledged id is read back from the final primary), 0 duplicated or missing feed effects, and ambiguous commits resolved by the oracle. Writes accepted but never acknowledged in F2a / F2b are reported as their own number, never as a loss.
  • The primary metric: the client-visible write outage, from the fault to the first acknowledged write after it, per writer thread; the median over the 8 threads is used, and the max is reported. For F3, a planned switchover with no fault instant that stops writes, the compared number is the median over the threads of the longest gap between acknowledged writes ending in (the fault, the end of the hold]; the literal number is published too.
  • Acknowledged means the database confirmed durability: for MongoDB, no write-concern error; for PostgreSQL, the transaction waited for the synchronous standby.
  • Also reported: detection, promotion and rediscovery where observable; the longest single write; the time to a resumed feed; the time to full redundancy; false failovers in a 10-minute steady phase; etcd fsync latency.
  • Moving parts: EXP 020's component checklist, counted in kinds for the verdict, with processes published too. Data members cancel on both sides, and defaults are not components.

An even setup

  • MongoDB: 3 data members, priority 1 / votes 1, no arbiter; keyFile + auth; 2 GB and 2 CPU per member; writes {w: "majority", j: true}; PyMongo 4.10 with retryable writes and reads, timeoutMS 5000. Default timing: electionTimeoutMillis 10000 / heartbeatIntervalMillis 2000; tuned: 5000 / 1000.
  • PostgreSQL: 3 nodes, each running PG16, Patroni (etcd3: API) and an etcd member; 2 GB and 2 CPU per node; synchronous_mode: quorum (ANY 1), with classic sync mode as a variant; data checksums + use_pg_rewind; libpq multi-host with target_session_attrs=read-write; writes idempotent by proof id. Default timing: ttl 30 / loop_wait 10 / retry_timeout 10; tuned: 20 / 4 / 8, the most aggressive documented, supported setting. Patroni's validator floor, 20 / 1 / 3, runs as a labelled line.
  • The workload: proof records of about 2 KB at 20 a second from 8 writer threads, with ids chosen by the client; one shared retry loop for both arms (the same id, 0.5 s backoff, a 90 s budget).
  • The feed: effect and cursor in one transaction on both arms; a MongoDB change stream against the PostgreSQL commit-order-safe outbox, with pgoutput on Patroni's permanent logical slots as a second variant. One variant is compared per scenario.
  • The host: Windows + WSL2 Docker, two networks, named volumes, run only when no other benchmark measures.

How a verdict is decided

  • A fault verdict uses the gate and the outage only: mongodb-fits or postgres-fits when the gate passes and the median outage is at least 20% shorter than the other arm's compared variant in at least 4 of 5 paired repetitions, with the same direction in the reproduction on a second seed.
  • The compared PostgreSQL variant is one per (timing, fault): among the variants that pass the gate in every cell on both seeds, the one with the lowest median of its seed-1 repetition medians.
  • The moving-parts verdict is its own line: at least 2 fewer component kinds.
  • Only one arm passes the gate: that arm fits, labelled gate-only. Otherwise both-fit, neither or invalid. A false failover in an arm's steady cell at a timing means that arm cannot get a -fits verdict at that timing.
  • Repetitions: 5 per (arm, variant, cell, fault) on seed 1, plus a reproduction of every cell on seed 2. Every cell, variant and outcome is published.

The four reviews

Each reviewer was an independent model reviewer (Claude, fresh context), briefed to give its store its best realistic HA design and to veto strawmen. The first round reviewed the design draft and filed 16 vetoes: among them, the topology (three data members, no arbiter), matched client timeouts and one shared retry loop, a node-level kill on both arms, a second network so a partition can show split-brain, quorum sync, and rejoin hygiene. The second round reviewed 12 open questions the runner surfaced and filed 6 more: among them, PostgreSQL's tuned timing (20 / 4 / 8 rather than the validator floor), F3's compared number, one compared PostgreSQL variant instead of a best-of-two per repetition, and false failovers counting against the arm that has them.

What this does not establish

Named next, not judged here: multi-host networks, sharding, Kubernetes operators, and HAProxy or pg_auto_failover as primary arms. Everything runs on one Docker host, so it says nothing about failover across machines.
