EXP 015: Does Odin's cost estimate match what the brokers bill?

RESEARCH NOTE / 013 · EXP 015 · PRE-REGISTERED, MEASURED 28 OCT – 3 NOV 2026

Odin estimates the cost of every model call from a price book, before the broker's bill arrives. Before any savings figure can be published, that estimate has to be checked against what the brokers actually charge. EXP 015 sent one identical tiny completion through each of three brokers, OpenRouter, EUrouter and Requesty, every 3 hours for 7 whole UTC days, and compared the price-book estimate with the cost each broker reported, call by call.

Measured over 336 counted calls across 7 days: the estimate held for OpenRouter and Requesty and was off by a stable 10% factor for EUrouter, so a savings claim is honest broker by broker, not fleet-wide; the fleet-wide half was not measurable from this lane, which lacks the private routing log fleet.json needs. Every one of the 336 attempted calls succeeded; none were imputed.

What was fixed in advance

The question, the three brokers, the two models, the capture cadence, the per-broker and fleet decision rules were published before the window opened and frozen by sha256; the record was merged 23 October 2026, and the counted window ran 2026-10-28T00:00Z to 2026-11-04T00:00Z, with billing read no earlier than 2026-11-05T00:00Z to let OpenRouter's per-call figures settle.

  • Models: anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5 and openai/gpt-4o-mini, each under each broker's own id, one call per model per broker every 3 hours (56 runs, 6 calls each).
  • Metric: the relative gap r = brokerReportedCost ÷ priceBookEstimate − 1, for every counted call (inside the window, HTTP 200, both a broker cost and a positive estimate).
  • Decision rule, per broker: "estimate holds" if the median |r| is at most 5% and its 95% interval sits inside ±5%; "off by a stable factor" if not holding but the interval is at most 2 points wide; otherwise "unreliable". Fewer than 50 counted calls reports as "not enough calls".
  • Decision rule, fleet: do Odin's own logged estimates cover what OpenRouter billed the account over the same window — a ratio between 0.9 and 1.1 with at most 10% zero-estimate rows "supports a savings claim"; otherwise, or if not computed, it does not.

Every counted call carried a broker-reported usage.cost (in-band share 100% for all three), so there is nothing imputed and nothing missing. The gap was exactly the same on every one of the 7 counted days for every broker: 0% for OpenRouter and Requesty, 10% for EUrouter, which is why each 95% interval collapses to a single point. EUrouter served both models through other infrastructure under its own id (aws-bedrock/claude-haiku-4.5 and microsoft-foundry/gpt-4o-mini) rather than the model vendor's own API; its 10% markup matches its own published catalogue pricing, exactly as the pre-registration's calibration run predicted. Of the 112 OpenRouter calls, 109 also carried a generation record to cross-check against usage.cost, and all 109 agreed within 1%.

What the fleet half could not answer

The pre-registration's second question — whether Odin's own logged estimates, summed over the window, cover what OpenRouter actually billed the account — needs fleet.json: counts and a ratio computed privately from Odin's routing log and OpenRouter's account activity, by a script (fleet-cost-reconcile.mjs) that runs against production billing data this lane does not hold. No fleet.json was available to this record, so the frozen analysis reports it as it is designed to: not measurable, rather than guessed or left silently absent. Whoever can run that reconciliation next should treat this as open, not as "supports" by default.

What it means

Measured: for these two models, on list-priced, uncached, tiny calls, Odin's price-book estimate is a usable stand-in for OpenRouter and Requesty's bill with no correction. For EUrouter it needs a known, stable correction (multiply by 1.10) before a savings claim built on estimates would be honest. Not measured: discounts, cached calls, long prompts, reasoning tokens, or whether the fleet's logged estimates as a whole track what was actually billed.

Evidence and its boundary

Every counted call is published: the results, recomputed in CI by the frozen analysis script from the capture rows themselves, copied unchanged from the scheduled GitHub Actions runs that produced them. No absolute dollar figures are published or were computed here: the metric is a ratio, by design, before the first counted call ran. The capture code that calls the brokers is private; its files are frozen by the sha256 the pre-registration states, which a reader can check without running it.

Pre-registered experiment. One laptop reads and analyses published rows; nothing ran locally. Field note written 28 Nov 2026, after the window's artifacts were pulled from the 90-day-retained capture runs.
