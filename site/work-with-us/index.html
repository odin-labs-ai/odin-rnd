What we take on.

Hard problems where “it seems to work” is not good enough.

  • LEGACY CODE

    Refactor and migrate

    Move old systems to new code without changing what they do, and prove it with checks against an independent expectation, not just against the old output. See the Migration Witness.

  • SECURITY

    Fix it and show it holds

    Find and fix weaknesses, then show that the fix holds and that the checks around it still catch what they should. See the CI Witness.

  • FRONTIER EXPERIMENTS

    Space, robotics, vision, ML

    New directions we take on with partners. Each one starts as a written experiment: the question, the data and what would count as success, fixed before anything runs.

  • BLUEPRINTS

    Rules enforced as code

    Write your architecture rules down once and have code check them on every change, with the open-source Blueprint Conformance Engine.

How an engagement runs.

Scoped together, by proposal. No price list: every engagement is different.

  3. 03

    Work in stages

    Over months, in stages. Each stage ends with evidence and a decision: continue, change course or stop.

The Agent Acceptance Bench

A proposed engineering engagement around one real acceptance problem. Bring a change your team struggles to trust: generated tests, a behavior-sensitive upgrade or a repair that keeps coming back. We build the explicit contract and reproducible checks with your reviewers.

Scope and terms agreed together. No performance outcome promised.

A useful first question beats a broad rollout.

This is for engineering teams with a recurring task, an existing way to do it, and someone who can judge the result. A maintenance change, a test-writing task or a release review could be a starting point.

You bring approved, representative samples, a baseline and a named human reviewer. Together we choose a narrow workflow, define acceptance criteria and decide which failures would make us stop.

If the workflow or ownership is still unclear, the first conversation is about finding that boundary.

From a question to a decision.

Three phases, with review built into the work.

  1. 01

    Scope the work

    Agree the task, samples, acceptance criteria and reviewer. Record access boundaries, exclusions and the conditions for stopping before a run begins.

  2. 02

    Compare with a baseline

    Use an agreed evaluation to compare the current approach with agent-assisted work. Review the outputs and count tool cost, human review and rework.

  3. 03

    Choose what happens next

    Inspect failures and limitations alongside accepted results. Decide together whether to continue, change the approach or stop, then hand over the record.

Something your team can inspect and keep.

The proposed handover makes the reasoning behind the decision visible.

A repeatable evaluation
The task definition, approved sample references, acceptance criteria and instructions for another run.
A reviewed baseline comparison
Results from both approaches, the reviewer’s decisions, failures and limits of the comparison.
A total-cost account
Tool use, human review and rework, with assumptions and gaps recorded.
A portable handover
A readable report and agreed artifacts that your team can use to make its next decision.

Inspect the public experiments

Blueprint Conformance Engine checks code against explicit architecture constraints. Our Witness experiments examine a different part of acceptance: whether a test catches a known defect, whether two implementations share a bug, and whether a correction preserves the checks that could reject it.

Inspect the concrete cases: a green test that misses a rounding defect, two versions agreeing on the wrong answer, and a fix that passes its target but fails a regression. These are reproducible authored fixtures, not customer outcome claims.

Read the experiment data and its source metadata. Agent markets and robotics remain future research directions, rather than products offered through this collaboration.
